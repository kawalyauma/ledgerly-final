Ledgerly Academic System

The academic system Ledgerly AI runs. It builds schemes of work and notes from real curriculum material, plans every lesson onto the timetable, scans exercise books and lesson plan books, keeps a grouped question bank per class and term, tracks what each class is being taught, and keeps an evidence-based profile of each learner.

What the system doesOne academic engine on top of Ledgerly, with Ledgerly AI as the master brain

📚 Builds the teaching content

Ledgerly AI reads the national curriculum, existing schemes of work, notes and past exams from the e-library. From them it writes the school's own schemes of work, then the notes and activities for each lesson.

🗓️ Plans every period

It manages the timetable and splits each lesson into the periods it needs. It then places those periods on the term calendar, so you know which subtopic is taught on which day in every class.

📷 Reads the books

A scanner app photographs exercise books and lesson plan books in batches. The AI reads every page: questions, answers, the teacher's marks, handwriting, spelling and language.

❓ Grows a question bank

Questions from the sources and from learners' books are stored per class and term, and similar questions are grouped (1 + 2 and 2 + 4 fall in one group). Teachers can compare a question with the bank and get alternative questions to give learners.

📍 Knows what is taught now

It compares the plan (timetable and period plan) with the evidence (lesson plan books, exercise books, teacher entries). It shows what every class is learning at any moment and which classes are behind.

🧒 Knows each learner

Each learner's overview shows handwriting, spelling level, language, presentation, results per subject, weak skills, common mistakes and achievements. Every point comes from the learner's own scanned work.

Ground rules

1 · Nothing is invented

The AI may only use the passages, page images and records it is given. Every unit, lesson and note cites the passages it came from. A question enters the bank only if its exact wording is found in the source or on the scanned page. Sums are checked by the system, not taken from the AI. If the material doesn't cover something, the AI reports a gap instead of filling it in.

2 · Ledgerly AI writes the schemes

Schemes already stored in Ledgerly, and those in the e-library, are reference material only. Ledgerly AI composes the school's scheme from the curriculum, schemes of work, notes and past exams together.

3 · Step by step, not all at once

Large PDFs are never sent whole. The AI first writes the overall scheme, then works one lesson at a time, each step reading only the few passages relevant to that lesson. This keeps usage low and lets you pause, stop or switch provider between any two steps.

4 · People approve

Everything the AI produces starts as a draft. The DOS or teacher reviews it, corrects it and publishes it. Pages the AI is unsure about (for example a learner's name it can't match) go to a review list.

Ledgerly AI: the master brain AIOne engine runs every AI job in the academic system

Every piece of AI work is a small, recorded step in a queue. The Ledgerly AI dashboard shows the queue and gives the owner these controls:

Provider switch

Choose Claude or Codex for writing schemes, lessons and summaries. Switching takes effect from the next step. Reading scanned page images uses the image-capable provider.

Start · Pause · Stop

Pause lets the current step finish and takes no new ones. Stop also interrupts the step in progress. Nothing is lost: queued work continues when you press Start.

Usage limits

A daily step limit per school and a maximum prompt size per step. The dashboard shows today's steps, time used and the size of every prompt.

Full history

Every step records its provider, duration, size, result and any error. Failed steps retry automatically with a pause between tries. One failed lesson never blocks the rest of the scheme.

Kinds of step: collect sources · scheme outline · write one lesson · read one scanned page · learner summary · (planned) build timetable · plan periods · extract a past paper into the bank.

Scheme of work builder AISourcesFrom curriculum and resources to a full scheme with notes for every lesson

  1. Choose class, subject and termThe DOS picks, for example, Primary Four · Social Studies · Term 3. The system maps it to the e-library automatically (p4 · social-studies · term-3) and lets you add or remove resources by hand.
  2. Collect the materialIt gathers the curriculum, two or three schemes of work, lesson plans, notes and past papers for that class and subject. Each resource's text is stored once and split into short, citable passages. No AI is used in this step.
  3. Overall scheme (AI)The AI combines the curriculum and the reference schemes into the school's scheme: themes and topics in order, week ranges, competences, and the list of lessons with subtopics and objectives. Anything it can't trace to a passage is dropped.
  4. Lessons, one by one (AI)For each lesson the AI receives only the passages on that lesson. It writes learner notes, teaching methods, materials, life skills and assessment, and lists the activities found in the sources. Verified questions go straight into the question bank.
  5. Review and publishThe DOS reads the scheme, edits or asks for a rewrite of any lesson, and publishes it. Published schemes feed the period planner, the teaching tracker and the kiosk.

Timetable manager & period planner PlannedFrom lessons to "which subtopic is taught on which day"

1 · Build the timetable

  • Inputs: classes and streams, subjects with periods per week, teacher assignments, rooms, the daily bell (period times, breaks, lunch).
  • Rules: no teacher or room double-booked, a limit on periods per day for each subject, double periods where needed (e.g. science practicals), core subjects in the morning.
  • The planner proposes a timetable, flags clashes, and lets the DOS swap slots by hand before publishing.

2 · Split lessons into periods

  • Each scheme lesson gets a length in periods, taken from the source scheme ("WK 1, PD 1–2") or estimated from its content.
  • Long lessons are split into parts: Plant life — Part 1: types of plants and Part 2: parts of a flowering plant.
  • Revision, tests and practical periods are kept where the scheme has them.

3 · Place periods on the calendar

  • Walks the term day by day: timetable slots minus public holidays, school events, exam weeks and half-term.
  • Result: every class has a dated plan, for example Tue 14 Oct, P4, 10:20–11:00, SST: Our district, Physical features — mountains and hills.
  • Teachers get their week's plan; the kiosk and AI chat read the same plan.

4 · Re-plan automatically

  • When the scanned books or teacher entries show a lesson was missed or not finished, the rest of the plan moves forward.
  • If a subject falls behind, it warns how many periods are needed to catch up before exams.

Question bank BuiltPer class, per term, grouped, and always traceable

Where questions come from

  • E-library: exercises, examples and past paper items found while writing lessons, plus whole past papers (planned).
  • Scanned books: every activity a teacher gave, copied exactly from learners' exercise books.
  • Each question keeps its origin: the resource and passage, or the scanned page.

Grouping

  • Sums are grouped by structure: 1 + 2, 2 + 4, "Add 3 and 4" and "the sum of 6 and 2" all fall in Addition of 1-digit numbers; 23 + 45 falls in Addition of 2-digit numbers.
  • Other questions are grouped by the subtopic they test and the skill (identify, explain, compute…).
  • Each group shows how many questions it has, how hard they are, and how learners score on it.

Compare and offer alternatives

  • Compare: type or pick a question to see whether it is already in the bank, the same-type questions, and similar wording.
  • Alternatives: other questions from the same group, least used first, skipping ones a given learner has already done, optionally harder.
  • Practice sets: an exercise for a class, a lesson or one learner, focused on that learner's weakest groups.

Scanner app App builtBackend builtFast batch scanning of books, then automatic reading

  1. Start a batchChoose the type (learners' exercise books, lesson plan book, exam scripts, teacher notes), the class, the subject and the date.
  2. Scan quicklyPoint the phone at each page. The app detects the page edges, crops, straightens and sharpens it, and captures automatically when steady. You can tap a learner's name before their pages, or let the AI read the name on the page.
  3. Works offlinePages queue on the phone and upload when the connection is good. Re-sending a page never creates a duplicate, and the same image scanned twice is detected.
  4. AI reads each pageIt records the page type, learner's name and date, topic, each question with the learner's answer and the teacher's tick or cross, corrections, handwriting, spelling mistakes, language, presentation and teacher comments. Only what is visible is recorded; unreadable words are marked as illegible.
  5. Records updateThe learner is matched to the class list, the questions join the bank, answers and observations join the learner's profile, and the topic joins the teaching timeline. Pages with an unclear name go to a short review list.

How it is built

One app, one sign-in

  • Same Ledgerly login as the scanner. Tabs and actions follow the user's role: the DOS sees Coverage and Schemes, a teacher sees their own classes.
  • Same app ID, so phones with the scanner update in place and keep unsent pages.

Native Android, modern UI

  • Kotlin with Jetpack Compose and Material 3, using the reference style: Ledgerly blue #1D5FD1, white 16 px cards, a gradient featured card, round icon chips and progress rings.
  • The scanner's capture, offline store and upload worker are kept as they are.

Works on weak connections

  • Today's lessons, notes, drawings and timetable are cached for offline reading.
  • "Lesson taught" ticks and scans queue offline and send later.
  • Images are loaded small first. Files are kept in NexDrive.

Small backend additions

  • GET /learn/me: my classes, subjects, today's periods and counts for Home and Profile.
  • GET /learn/search: one search across lessons, learners and questions.
  • Later: push notifications (a period starting, a scheme ready, scans to review).

Build order

  1. 1 · Shell and designCompose theme, the five-tab bar, the new sign-in, Home with Now teaching, and Profile. The scanner moves in as the Scan tab. /learn/me added.
  2. 2 · Lessons and timetableClasses → scheme → lesson list → lesson page with notes, drawings, print and share. Mark as taught. My week.
  3. 3 · Question bankSearch, groups, alternatives, photo-compare, practice sets, labelling quizzes from the image library.
  4. 4 · Learners and AILearner overview, summary, weak-skill practice. Ask Ledgerly AI. DOS coverage and schemes.
  5. 5 · Offline and releaseCaching, notifications, testing on the school phones, signed APK and an install link.

Teaching tracker BuiltPlan versus reality, for every class

Right now

For any date and time: the subject on the timetable for each class, the latest topic actually taught in each subject, the matching scheme lesson, and the next lesson.

Timeline

A dated history per class and subject, with the evidence behind each entry: lesson plan book, exercise book or teacher entry.

Coverage

Per subject: lessons in the scheme, lessons taught, where the class should be by this week, and how far behind it is.

Learner overview Built"Give me Brian's academic overview"

Writing

  • Handwriting: rating, legibility, letter formation, spacing, trend (improving, steady or declining).
  • Spelling: level and the words they misspell most, as written.
  • Language: languages written, grammar issues, sentence construction.
  • Presentation: neatness, margins, crossing out.

Learning

  • Accuracy per subject from marked work.
  • Weaknesses: skill groups below 60%, and the usual mistakes (carrying, misreading…).
  • Achievements: skills mastered, and skills that have clearly improved.
  • Teacher comments, newest first.

Summary and next steps

  • A short AI write-up for the head teacher or parents, written only from these figures, which says when there is too little evidence.
  • A practice set built from the learner's weak groups.

A term in practice

  1. Before the termThe DOS orders schemes for every class and subject; Ledgerly AI writes them step by step overnight. The DOS reviews and publishes them, then builds the timetable, and the planner places every lesson on the calendar.
  2. Every dayTeachers follow the period plan. The kiosk shows what each class is learning now.
  3. After lessons and testsBooks are scanned in batches. The system learns which topics were really covered, adds the activities to the bank and updates each learner's profile.
  4. Every weekLagging subjects are re-planned, and teachers get practice sets for weak skills.
  5. End of termLearner overviews and summaries are ready for reports and parents.

Security and privacy

  • Every record belongs to one school; no school can see another's data.
  • Permissions: read, write, scan and engine control are separate; only owners and admins can switch or stop the AI.
  • Scanned images are stored privately and only shown to signed-in staff.
  • The AI receives only what a step needs: passages for a lesson, or one page image.
  • The full-text link to the e-library uses a private key between Ledgerly and ULibTech.
  • Every AI step is logged and can be audited.
